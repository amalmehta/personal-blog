*This is a sample post showing how math looks.*

Something that grows by a rate $r$ each step, starting from $x_0$, reaches

$$
x_n = x_0 (1 + r)^n
$$

after $n$ steps. The time it takes to double doesn't depend on where you start:

$$
n_{\text{double}} = \frac{\ln 2}{\ln(1 + r)} \approx \frac{0.693}{r}
$$

At 7% per step that's about ten steps. The first doubling adds one $x_0$; the fifth adds sixteen. The rate never changed. The amount being multiplied did.
